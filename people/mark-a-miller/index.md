I build [LinkML](https://linkml.io) schemas, ontologies and metadata standards that let biological and environmental data be combined and reused, currently for [DOE BRIDGE](https://doe-bridge.github.io/), which builds schemas and tooling for the data lakehouses of DOE's Biological and Environmental Research (BER) program. That means defining the classes and slots, adding examples that validate, mapping slots to terms in ontologies such as the [Environment Ontology (ENVO)](https://github.com/EnvironmentOntology/envo), and checking real data against the result.

Current projects: BRIDGE and its [central schema](https://github.com/doe-bridge/bridge-central-schema), [nmdc-schema](https://github.com/microbiomedata/nmdc-schema) for the [National Microbiome Data Collaborative](/project/nmdc/), [BERIL](/project/BERIL/), LinkML itself, and the [MIxS](https://github.com/GenomicsStandardsConsortium/mixs) sequence metadata standards. For [OPAL](https://opal-doe.org/), DOE's Orchestrated Platform for Autonomous Laboratories, I am describing Joint BioEnergy Institute (JBEI) lab data in LinkML.

I joined BBOP in March 2021. Before that I generated biological data at the bench and analyzed it in industry. From 2017 to 2021 I was a biomedical ontologist at the University of Pennsylvania's Institute for Biomedical Informatics, using ontologies so that clinical records could be combined and reused. Metadata standards for environmental and microbiome data are the same problem, which is why I work on them now.
